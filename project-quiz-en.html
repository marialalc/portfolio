Exploring a retention strategy

  • Year: 2025
  • My role: Lead designer
  • Type: Pilot test
  • Platforms: Web and newsletter

Validating a product idea through
real user signals

Why it needed to be done

For Luci to help users adopt healthy habits, they need to use the app regularly. However, retention was low and few users were returning to the platform.

This problem arose at a time when development resources were committed to infrastructure updates, making any app modifications unrealistic.

Initiative

Identify user re-engagement levers while mobilizing little to no development resources.

When engagement drops, how do you rekindle interest?

Our experience map revealed a marked decline after creating a first goal. This drop-off happened exactly when we wanted users to consume health-related content created by Luci.

Content goal

Create short, digestible content

Business goal

Increase retention

Thematic quizzes to inform and engage users, while increasing retention.

I seized the opportunity to combine two initiatives into one solution. Creating thematic quizzes would allow us to produce short content while encouraging user re-engagement.

Digging into the foundations

But developing quizzes on our platform would require significant effort, and development resources were limited. Before investing time in it, we needed a clear signal that the initiative was worthwhile.

To do this, I identified the hypotheses we needed to validate.

From idea to test in one week

How to test our hypotheses without writing a line of code? Using already available tools, I set up an temporary version of the quiz to test three of the four formulated hypotheses.

Retention couldn't be measured outside of the application, but this approach still allowed us to validate user interest in the format.

Desirability

Our users are interested in quizzes

Behavior

Our users will complete the quizzes

Satisfaction

Our users will enjoy the quizzes

1

I reached out to our experts and the Content team. Within two days, we developed 7 questions for our pilot quiz.

2

Using Typeform, a survey creation platform, I created a pilot quiz with a scoring system and real-time validation.

3

To reach as many people as possible, I proposed adding the link to our pilot quiz in our June 2025 newsletter.

High engagement, strong satisfaction

Over the weekend, a large number of newsletter subscribers clicked on the quiz and completed it. Their behavior allowed us to answer our research questions.

Are users interested in this content?

11%

Click-through rate on the newsletter CTA

✅ Yes. The click-through rate matched or exceeded that of other CTAs in the newsletter, indicating that users were curious and willing to interact with this type of content.

Do users complete the quiz?

4.4%

Bounce rate on the quiz landing page

100%

Quiz completion rate

✅ Yes. The completion and bounce rates indicated that the quiz was easy to follow and engaging enough to be completed.

Do users like the quiz ?

✅ Yes. The vast majority of users rated the quiz positively, indicating that the experience was not only functional, but also genuinely enjoyable.

Next steps

This experiment gave us a clear signal: thematic quizzes have the potential to bring users back to the app.

Next step: test whether quizzes activate usage of key features. We'll encourage users to take a core action (e.g., create a goal) and track the conversion rate.

If the results are conclusive, quizzes could become a key lever in our retention strategy.
