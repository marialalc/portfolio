Creating Luci’s Goal-Setting Feature

  • Year: 2024-2025
  • My role: Lead designer
  • Type: End to end
  • Platforms: Web (mobile and desktop)

Empowering users to take action independently

Why it needed to be done

Luci supports its users in their behavior change. A key element is goal setting, which until now was done exclusively with the help of a human advisor.

But relying on advisors wasn't viable at scale, and users wanted more autonomy. With only 3% of users choosing the advisor, we were missing opportunities to have a concrete impact on their habits.

Initiative

Increase the app's impact by allowing users to set their goals autonomously, while building on the advisors' methodology.

We talked to users about change...

We targeted users in the contemplation stage of behavior change: people who knew they needed change but were hesitant to start.

We learned that they...

  • Were overwhelmed by their busy schedules
  • Believed that change requires drastic effort
  • Were discouraged by their past failures
  • Lacked confidence to maintain their habits

...and we shadowed advisors

By observing advisors in action, we identified the pillars of their approach:

  • Breaking the ice: Advisors instinctively suggested simple, low-demand actions: small achievable steps, regardless of the goal.
  • Prioritizing action over perfection: Advisors didn't force radical changes, but focused on one small achievable action each week.
  • Letting structure emerge naturally: Advisors let goals define themselves through experience. Once users found effective strategies, they gradually transformed them into structured goals.

From field research to a first concept

As a team, we gathered our learnings and designed an adapted experience.

A trial and collect approach would allow users to adopt new habits more naturally. Instead of starting with structured goals, they would pick from a bank of micro-goals, test them, then keep the ones that work in a toolbox.

We created and launched a first iteration

Would users understand the concepts involved? Would they create goals? With this first version, we could observe their behavior at scale.

From clicks to conversations

Within 2 months of going into production, only 9% of users had created at least one goal, a low adoption rate for a key feature.

Among those who started adding a goal, only 21% completed the process.

To understand the why behind these results, we launched a series of user tests with people unfamiliar with our application.

Our mission: observe users during goal creation and understand the barriers and friction points.

  • The vocabulary was unfamiliar
  • The journey was too complex

How might we...

Increase the completion rate of adding goals?

Make the goal experience easier to understand?

Removing barriers to action

The first step was to rework the "My Goals" page. With its navigation and lack of guidance, it wasn't a good starting point for the experience.

The navigation bar was poorly understood and several users missed it completely.

The choice between creating a goal alone or with an advisor generated unnecessary mental load.

Navigation is improved with sections indicating the domain.

Self-management is prioritized while keeping the advisor option visible.

Next, we needed to rework the experience's architecture. The toolbox concept was misunderstood and made the structure too complex. So we simplified the structure by removing its dedicated page.

The toolbox confused goals and added layers of navigation.

The list of goals to try was rarely used and added clutter.

Instead of the toolbox, we opted for a simple view of each goal.

Removing barriers to action

The first step was to rework the "My Goals" page. With its navigation and lack of guidance, it wasn't a good starting point for the experience.

  • The navigation bar was poorly understood and several users missed it completely → We replaced it with sections indicating the domain.
  • The choice between creating a goal alone or with an advisor generated unnecessary mental load → We opted for an implicit self-guided choice while keeping the advisor option visible.

Next, we needed to rework the experience's architecture. The toolbox concept was misunderstood and made the structure too complex. So we simplified the structure by removing its dedicated page.

  • The toolbox confused goals and added layers of navigation → We replaced it with a simple view of each goal.
  • The list of goals to try was rarely used and added clutter. → We removed it from the experience.

Second iteration

This version of the experience stays true to the concept of trying goals through micro-actions. But the process and architecture have been greatly simplified:

  • The toolbox concept no longer exists,
  • Navigation and the number of steps have been reduced,
  • The interface is streamlined to improve accessibility.

Human expertise doesn't always translate directly into an interface. Sometimes we need to simplify a concept to make it usable.

The impact of this change

Within three months of launch, the completion rate for adding a goal increased from 21% to 43%.

Change in goal addition completion rate

21% to 43%

By making goals more accessible and self-managed, we enabled more users to take action on their habits.
